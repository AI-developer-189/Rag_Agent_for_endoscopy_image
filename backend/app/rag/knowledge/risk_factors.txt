# Gastrointestinal Risk Factors Reference

## Patient Age Risk Stratification
- **Under 40**: Lower baseline risk; consider alternative diagnoses first
- **40-60**: Average risk; standard screening guidelines apply
- **60+**: Elevated risk for adenocarcinoma, polyps, and IBD mimicry; more aggressive surveillance warranted

## Lifestyle Risk Factors
- **Tobacco use**: Increases risk of gastric cancer, esophageal cancer, peptic ulcer disease
- **Alcohol consumption**: >2 drinks/day increases risk of esophageal, gastric, and colorectal cancer
- **Diet high in processed/red meat**: Associated with increased colorectal cancer risk
- **Low fiber diet**: Associated with increased colorectal cancer risk
- **Obesity**: Increased risk of esophageal adenocarcinoma, colorectal cancer
- **Sedentary lifestyle**: Associated with increased colorectal cancer risk

## Medication Risk Factors
- **NSAIDs/Sulfonamides**: Increased risk of gastric ulcer, small bowel injury, colonic ulceration
- **Corticosteroids**: Increased risk of peptic ulcer, especially with NSAID co-use
- **Aspirin**: Low-dose aspirin increases gastrointestinal bleeding risk
- **Bisphosphonates**: Pill-induced esophageal injury with poor adherence to upright positioning
- **Iron supplements**: Can cause gastric mucosal irritation, mimic gastritis

## Family History Risk Factors
- **First-degree relative with colorectal cancer**: 2x increased risk; start screening 10 years before relative's diagnosis age
- **Family history of familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)**: Early surveillance required (pediatric onset)
- **Family history of Lynch syndrome**: Increased risk of colorectal and endometrial cancer; microsatellite instability testing
- **Family history of IBD**: Increased risk of UC or Crohn's disease mimicry

## Endoscopic Findings with Higher Risk
- **Large polyps ≥10mm**: Higher malignant potential; may require EMR rather than simple polypectomy
- **Sessile serrated polyposis**: Increased risk of right-sided colorectal cancer; intensive surveillance needed
- **Polyps with high-grade dysplasia**: Upgrade surveillance interval to 3 years or less
- **Non-lifting sign**: May indicate submucosal invasion; surgical referral required
- **Barrett's esophagus columnar-lined mucosa ≥3cm**: Enhanced surveillance every 3 years with biopsies

## Clinical Decision Support Triggers
- Age ≥50 with new-onset GI symptoms: Lower threshold for endoscopy
- Unexplained iron-deficiency anemia: Urgent GI evaluation
- Positive fecal occult blood test (gFIT): Follow-up colonoscopy recommended
- Family history + age >40: Earlier and more frequent screening
- Long-standing UC (>8-10 years): Initiate colorectal cancer surveillance
