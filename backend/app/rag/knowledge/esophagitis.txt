# Esophagitis - Clinical Guidelines

## Overview
Esophagitis is inflammation of the esophageal mucosa, often caused by gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), medications, or infections. Classification uses the Los Angeles (LA) system: Grade A (one or more mucosal breaks <5mm), Grade B (mucosal breaks >5mm), Grade C (confluent mucosal breaks involving <75% of circumference), Grade D (confluent mucosal breaks involving >=75% of circumference).

## Risk Factors
- Chronic GERD or acid reflux
- Hiatal hernia
- Obesity
- Smoking
- Alcohol consumption
- NSAIAs and aspirin use
- Scleroderma and other connective tissue diseases
- Hiatal hernia
- Zollinger-Ellison syndrome

## Clinical Guidelines

### Los Angeles Classification & Grading
- **Grade A**: Separate mucosal erosions ≤5 mm
- **Grade B**: Mucosal erosions >5 mm without continuous mucosal involvement
- **Grade C**: Confluent erosions involving <75% of circumferential mucosa
- **Grade D**: Confluent erosions involving ≥75% of circumferential mucosa

### PPI Therapy Guidelines
- **Initial treatment**: High-dose PPI (e.g., Pantoprazole 40mg IV BID or PO BID) for 4-8 weeks
- **Maintenance**: Low-dose PPI (e.g., Pantoprazole 20mg QD) after symptom resolution
- **Healing rates**: ~80-90% with 8 weeks of PPI therapy

### Endoscopic Surveillance
- **Erosive esophagitis**: Repeat endoscopy in 6-8 weeks after PPI therapy to confirm healing
- **Barrett's esophagus surveillance**: Every 3 years if Barrett's identified (≥3cm intestinal metaplasia)
- **No Barrett's, Grade A esophagitis**: Can repeat in 6 months or transition to surveillance every 3 years

### Treatment Algorithms
1. Empiric PPI trial for 4-8 weeks in typical reflux symptoms
2. If no response: H2 blocker add-on or biliary referral
3. If Barrett's identified: Surveillance endoscopy schedule based on intestinal metaplasia length
4. Consider H. pylori testing if PUD symptoms present
