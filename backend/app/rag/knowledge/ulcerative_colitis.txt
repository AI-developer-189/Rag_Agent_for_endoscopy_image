# Ulcerative Colitis - Clinical Guidelines

## Overview
Ulcerative Colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease characterized by continuous mucosal inflammation limited to the colon and rectum. Disease extent determines clinical classification and management.

## Disease Extent Classification
- **Ulcerative proctitis**: Inflammation limited to rectum (≤10cm from anal verge)
- **Proctosigmoiditis**: Inflammation involving rectum and sigmoid colon
- **Left-sided colitis**: Inflammation extending to splenic flexure (≤40cm)
- **Pancolitis**: Involvement of entire colon (>40cm)

## Risk Factors
- Onset typically age 15-30 or 50-70
- Familial aggregation (first-degree relatives have 5-10x increased risk)
- Jewish ancestry (higher prevalence)
- Smoking: paradoxically protective for UC (but increases Crohn's risk)
- Appendectomy early in life may increase risk

## Clinical Guidelines

### Mayo Score for Disease Activity
- **0**: Remission
- **1**: Mild (symptoms present but minimal)
- **2**: Moderate
- **3**: Severe (frequent bloody stools, systemic symptoms)

### Treatment by Disease Severity

#### Mild (Mayo Score 1)
- First-line: Oral 5-aminosalicylate (5-ASA) such as mesalamine
- Rectal therapy (enema or suppository) for proctitis
- Avoid NSAIDs and sulfonamides

#### Moderate (Mayo Score 2)
- First-line: Oral 5-ASA + rectal therapy
- Corticosteroids if 5-ASA inadequate
- Consider azathioprine/6-MP for steroid-sparing

#### Severe (Mayo Score 3)
- Hospital admission for IV corticosteroids or cyclosporine
- Consider anti-TNF therapy (infliximab, adalimumab) if steroid-refractory
- Early gastroenterology consultation
- Watch for toxic megacolon, colorectal cancer risk

### Extra-Intestinal Manifestations
- Arthralgias/arthritis (5-20% of patients)
- Primary sclerosing cholangitis (5-10% of UC patients)
- Skin manifestations (erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum)
- Ocular inflammation (uveitis, scleritis)
- Laboratory monitoring: CBC, CMP, LFTs every 3-6 months

### Surgical Indications
- Steroid-refractory severe UC
- Fulminant colitis with failure of medical therapy
- Toxic megacolon or perforation
- Dysplasia on surveillance histology
- Recurrent severe flares affecting quality of life

### Colorectal Cancer Surveillance
- After 8-10 years of pancolitis: surveillance colonoscopy every 1-3 years
- After 15-20 years of left-sided colitis: surveillance every 3-5 years
- Enhanced surveillance (every 1 year) if primary sclerosing cholangitis coexists
- Random biopsies every 10cm across colon + targeted biopsies of visible lesions
- Repeat colonoscopy with chromoendoscopy may improve dysplasia detection
